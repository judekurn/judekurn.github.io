### ❓ What is Sustainability Science?

#### 📚 Foundational Horizons & Definitions
*   **The Origin of Nachhaltigkeit (1713):** Coined by the Saxon mining administrator **Hans Carl von Carlowitz** in his treatise *Sylvicultura Oeconomica* during a devastating timber shortage: *“Harvest only as much wood as can be regenerated, so that a continuous, stable, and sustainable utilization remains.”*
*   **The Global Paradigm (Brundtland Report, 1987):** Scaled the principle globally as: *“Meeting the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.”*
*   **The Transdisciplinary Mandate (Kates et al., 2001):** Formulated the core objective: *“Understand the fundamental character of interactions between nature and society and to encourage those interactions along more sustainable trajectories.”*
*   **The Three-System Leveling (Komiyama & Takeuchi, 2006):** Defined the academic discipline as one that explicitly analyzes the complex links across three specific levels: **global systems** (biosphere), **social systems** (infrastructure, economy, and politics), and **human systems** (individual behavior and mindsets).

***

#### 🗺️ My Synthesis
For me, **Sustainability Science is a transdisciplinary field focused on understanding the fundamental character of interactions between nature and society to encourage those interactions along more sustainable trajectories.** 

Human socio-economic activities and environmental systems are deeply coupled, indivisible networks. They are co-determined, meaning development in one system promotes development in the other; likewise, devastation in one system drives devastation in the other. The core purpose of this science is to untangle this structural complexity and make sustainable pathways clearer, operational, and feasible.
