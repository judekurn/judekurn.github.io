1.2 Systems Mapping

Definition & Graph / Network Theory

Systems mapping represents how elements and relationships are structured inside a system so feedback dynamics and intervention effects can be analyzed clearly. In graph and network theory, a network consists of:

Vertices (Nodes)

Entities, stocks, or variables in the map—here labeled N1 through N6.

Edges (Links)

Connections linking pairs of vertices to represent causal or structural relationships.

Directed Graphs

Edges have arrows indicating direction from source to target rather than symmetric pairs.

Weighted Graphs

Edges carry weights (such as Weak, Medium, or Strong stroke thickness) indicating relative strength.

Direct vs. Indirect Causation

How single links combine into multi-step pathways across N1–N6.

Direct Causation: A single directed edge such as N1 (Partner Density) → N2 (Return Convenience) or N6 (Hoarded Cup Stock) → N5 (Available Circulating Inventory) acts directly from source to target.
Indirect Causation: A multi-link chain such as N1 → N2 → N3 (Adoption Volume) transmits change through one or more intermediate nodes.
Virtuous vs. Vicious Cycle: A reinforcing loop can operate as a virtuous cycle (e.g., expanding partner network and return convenience) or a vicious cycle (e.g., declining availability and adoption).

1.6 AI Rapid Prototyping

Using AI assistants to draft candidate loops and interactive prototypes quickly while verifying every edge, polarity, and empirical claim.

Rapid Prototyping strengths

  • Fast structural iteration: Translates case notes into candidate nodes (N1–N6) and feedback hypotheses quickly.
  • Interactive exploration: Builds step-through loop views and shock scenarios for classroom testing.
  • Error exposure: Helps surface wiring mismatches when comparing expected vs. traced feedback behavior.

Verification Discipline

  • Check every edge & sign: Confirm each arrow's direction, polarity (+ or −), and loop classification (R vs. B).
  • Verify facts & dates: Cross-check numbers and policy history against primary or cited sources.
  • Keep ownership clear: Treat AI drafts as starting hypotheses to audit, not final authority.

1.7 CREATE model

Structured prompting with the CREATE model, starter prompt containing the planted error (N5+ → N2 in B1), external AI links, and Step 3 fix to N5+ → N1.

CREATE Framework

C — Context

Define the RECUP reusable deposit context and system scope.

R — Role

Set the assistant role as a causal loop systems modeller.

E — Expectations

Specify desired outputs: edge lists, R/B classification, and propagation steps.

A — Actions

Instruct the model to trace loops R1 and B1 and test surges/shocks.

T — Constraints & Tone

Keep explanations concise, rigorous, and grounded in stated loop logic.

E — Evaluate & Refine

Audit the output, catch wiring flaws, and apply corrections in Step 3.
