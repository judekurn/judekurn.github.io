What is Entropy Theory in Economics?

A

The Second Law of Thermodynamics (ΔS > 0)

In closed systems, isolated energy continuously degrades from usable, highly organized state (low entropy) into dispersed, random thermal vibrations (high entropy). Thermal energy cannot be converted back into work with 100% efficiency.

B

Bioeconomic Insight (Georgescu-Roegen, 1971)

Economic production does not create physical matter or energy; it merely transforms low-entropy terrestrial stocks (fossil energy, minerals, topsoil) and solar flows into high-entropy environmental dissipation (waste heat, micro-particulates, greenhouse gases).

Historical Clarification & Circular Economy Critique

Nicholas Georgescu-Roegen was the premier academic critic of 100% circular economy models. He formulated the proposed "4th Law of Thermodynamics", suggesting that macroscopic matter degrades irreversibly through wear, friction, and spatial dispersion. Thus, complete closed-loop circularity is a physical impossibility.

Georgescu-Roegen's 4th Law of Thermodynamics

"Complete recycling of material closed systems is physically impossible at macroscopic scales." Because friction, wear, micro-diffusion, and chemical degradation continuously scatter matter, recovering 100% of dispersed matter requires infinite energy and generates exponential entropic thermal leaks elsewhere in the environment.
