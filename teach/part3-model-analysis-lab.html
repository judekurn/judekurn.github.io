Part 3: Model Analysis & Revisions (Lab)

Today's Question

Is Germany's RECUP system genuinely resilient under alternative climate and socioeconomic futures? Or does its success depend on assumptions that rarely hold in practice?

Learning Outcomes

At the end of this activity you should be able to:

  • Identify hidden assumptions within sustainability interventions.
  • Evaluate system resilience using systems thinking.
  • Modify causal assumptions using new evidence.
  • Compare system behaviour under different future conditions.
  • Critically assess whether circular economy systems remain viable under climate stress.

Scenario

Germany's RECUP network aims to reduce single-use takeaway cup waste through a reusable cup return system.

The first dashboard generated by AI provides a useful starting model. However, all models simplify reality. Your role as sustainability analysts is not to build dashboards. Your role is to identify assumptions, challenge them using evidence, and revise the model.

Step 2: Research the RECUP System

Spend 3-5 minutes investigating RECUP. Look for:

  • Consumer behaviour challenges
  • Reverse logistics issues
  • Washing and sanitisation requirements
  • Energy requirements
  • Water requirements
  • Policy barriers
  • Infrastructure constraints
  • Extreme weather vulnerabilities
  • Urban-rural adoption differences

Step 3: Challenge the Model Assumptions

Based on your research, identify assumptions that are missing from the original model. Then formulate new system rules representing these missing dynamics. A good system rule changes how the model behaves.

Examples:

  • Heatwaves reduce washing capacity by 25%.
  • Transport disruptions increase cup return delays.
  • Consumer participation drops when return locations are inconvenient.
  • Rising electricity prices increase operating costs.
  • Severe drought increases cleaning costs.
