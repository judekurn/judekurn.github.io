Part 3: Create-Destroy-Recreate (CDR) Lab

Background

RECUP is Germany's largest reusable takeaway cup and food-container network. Customers pay a small deposit, borrow a reusable cup from participating cafés and retailers, and return it to any partner location within the network. The model seeks to reduce single-use packaging waste through reuse, reverse logistics, and network effects.

In this lab, we will investigate how the RECUP system behaves when new assumptions, constraints, and external shocks are introduced into the model.

Step 3: Challenge Assumptions

Locate the following section inside the original prompt:

[STUDENTS: EDIT OR ADD YOUR CUSTOM RULES BELOW BEFORE RUNNING THIS PROMPT]

Rule 1 (Deposit Sensitivity): Increase deposit value from €1 to €2. Increases return speed by +30%, but reduces impulse takeaway adoption by -15%.
Rule 2 (Bakery Commuter Density): Prioritize morning bakery chain drop-offs (+50% bakery node density). Reduces return friction 2x faster than sit-down cafés.
Rule 3 (Local Sourcing Friction): Source 100% recycled Polypropylene within Germany. Increases manufacturing cost by +25%, but cuts embodied supply chain carbon by -40%.

[END STUDENT EDITABLE SECTION]

Revise, replace, or expand the existing rules based on your research findings. Do not assume the original rules are correct. Treat them as hypotheses to be tested and improved.

Examples:

  • Cup Hoarding: Increase the proportion of customers keeping RECUP cups as souvenirs from 5% to 20%, reducing return rates and increasing replacement cup demand.
  • Industrial Washing Constraint: Heatwaves above 35°C reduce industrial washing capacity by 25%, increasing turnaround time and reducing available cup stock.
  • Coffee Supply Shock: Severe droughts in major coffee-growing regions increase coffee bean prices by 40%, reducing café profit margins and slowing partner network growth.

Step 4: Recreate the Model

Run the modified prompt again. Recreate a revised model incorporating your new assumptions and compare how the system behaves.

Step 5: Analyse Resilience

Analyse how the revised system and assess how the system performs under SSP1-2.6 and SSP3-6.0 conditions.

  • Which new assumption produced the greatest change in system behaviour?
  • What became the dominant bottleneck or vulnerability?
  • Would you consider the RECUP system resilient under future climate uncertainty? Why?

Reflection

Many circular economy interventions appear successful when examined in isolation. Yet every system depends on material inputs, energy flows, infrastructure, human behaviour, and ecological conditions.

As you revised the RECUP model, did the system become more resilient, or did new constraints simply emerge elsewhere?

Which matters more for sustainability: optimizing individual components, or understanding how components interact as a system?
