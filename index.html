03 · Pedagogical Architecture

Systems Science, CLDs & Experiential Learning

Sustainability education is approached not as passive compliance or abstract checklist, but as a rigorous non-linear science—equipping STEM and humanities students alike with a shared analytical language to diagnose feedback loops and navigate real-world trade-offs.

Intervention Archetype A

Strengthening Virtuous Cycles

When positive feedback drives sustainable outcomes—such as eco-conscious guest awareness reinforcing community conservation investment—students identify external dampening nodes (e.g., invisible operations) and formulate interventions to remove bottlenecks and amplify visibility.

Classroom Application SSOC202 Overseas Consultancy (Mana Earthly Paradise, Bali) & COR-SSOC2234 Patagonia Systems Mapping Lab.
Pillar I · Systems Literacy

Bridging STEM & Humanities Cohorts

Grounds sustainability in Nonlinear Dynamics (Lorenz’s butterfly effect), Social-Ecological Systems (SES), and Socio-Technical Transitions (STT). Students move beyond linear precision using the Student-as-Teacher peer-instruction model, and the Sustainable Delta Game simulation.

COR-SSOC2234
Pillar II · Field Consultancy

Project-Based Learning

Cross-disciplinary student teams spend two weeks on-site in Ubud, Bali collaborating with Mana Earthly Paradise (a B-Corp eco-resort), neighboring farmers, and local communities. Co-created Causal Loop Diagrams inform actionable sustainability strategies presented directly to executive leadership.

SSOC202 PPPM301
Pillar III · Equity Walkshops

Leading for Inclusion

Experiential learning scaled to the urban neighborhood: 3-hour walking workshops in Singapore’s Chinatown immerse undergraduates in firsthand field observation of hyperlocal sustainability initiatives—demonstrating a core principle: if it is not inclusive, it is not sustainable.
